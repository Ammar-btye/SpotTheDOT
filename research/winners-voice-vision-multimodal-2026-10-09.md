## 3. Patterns
1. **Accessibility plus a sensor is the dominant winning formula.** Nine of the 31 rows serve blind, deaf, ALS, non-verbal or elderly users through a camera or microphone: BlinkAI, Iris, Ranger, PathSense, SignVerse, Gaze Link, ViddyScribe, Eye AAC Select and Lume. The already-logged Shepherd, SixthSense and VisionNav follow the same pattern. The camera or mic *replaces a missing sense*, and judges can feel that in a 3-minute demo.
2. **Smart glasses are the 2025–26 vision form factor.** Snap Spectacles won Snap's challenge twice at LA Hacks 2025 (Ghostface Cookah, Pulse) and won Most Creative at Cal Hacks 12 (Bob). Meta Ray-Bans appear in aesthetica, and PUSHPA ran on smart glasses. When glasses are the sponsor's own hardware, building on them is close to a free sponsor-track entry.
3. **The body as the controller wins "Creative" and "Overall" prizes.** MediaPipe pose and hand tracking power jiggle wiggle, Jarvis, BlinkAI, CharAIdes, and the already-logged kinemo (PennApps XXVI 1st Overall). These are cheap to build, run in-browser, and get the judge physically playing. CharAIdes is just Gemini vision guessing your charades, and it won Most Creative.
4. **Gemini Live is now the default real-time stack.** Bob, Wand, drone-copilot and ORION all stream audio and video frames over WebSocket into a Gemini Live session and act through tool calls. Judges of the Gemini Live Agent Challenge valued seamless multimodality (seeing, hearing and speaking at once), creative answers to technical limits, and real-world use.
5. **ElevenLabs category winners use voice as the interface, not as a feature.** Lingua Spatial speaks your surroundings in a target language, Lume talks seniors through the screen, Eye AAC Select gives a voice to people who can't speak, and GibberLink is about voice agents themselves. Plain "chatbot with TTS" entries do not show up among winners. Pep and Hearti pair ElevenLabs with vision.
6. **The physical-world action is the climax of the demo.** Examples include a drone that flies on command (drone-copilot), a printed diffusion photo (diffuji), a robot doing CPR (HeartStart), and drones flagging survivors (AERIS). Edge or on-device inference gets its own prizes (TreeHacks Edge AI: Iris, PUSHPA, SurviveX).
7. **How these live demos were staged:**
   - Demos were built to survive venue WiFi. Mira reported that "Stanford WiFi" kept killing its stream, and diffuji, Iris and SurviveX ran on-device.
   - Teams quote latency numbers as proof: Shepherd under 50 ms from obstacle to motor, MindPad under 200 ms for gestures.
   - Bob reuses its WebSocket connections and wrote its own retry and resume logic for Gemini Live sessions.
   - Many winners attach a backup video (ORION on YouTube and Vimeo) or a slide deck (Lume, Movemend, SixthSense on Canva or Google Slides), and Shepherd notes a filmed demo video.
   - PathSense exposed a live ngrok URL as its "Try it out" link.

**Takeaways for our Knight Hacks project**
1. **Target one main track plus one modality prize.** Build one sensory loop that is mic or camera in, model, then voice or physical action out, for one specific underserved user. That one build qualifies for both ElevenLabs (voice out) and Gemini (vision or Live in) without scattering features. MLH generally awards one category per project, so pick the stronger of the two as the target.
2. **Make the judge the user.** Gesture, pose or "point and speak" interactions (jiggle wiggle, Wand, CharAIdes) let the judge try it at the table, which helps creativity scores.
3. **Engineer for venue WiFi.** Use a phone hotspot, run local MediaPipe/YOLO where possible, pre-warm the Gemini Live session, and record a fallback video before judging starts.
4. **Show latency and reliability numbers on the slide** (end-to-end milliseconds, accuracy). Winners use them to prove the system is real.
5. **If sponsors bring hardware (Spectacles, Meta glasses, Jetson, drones), build on it.** Sponsor-hardware hacks win that sponsor's track at a high rate.
