# Winners: Education and EdTech track winners (2023–2026)
_Researched 2026-10-09 · sources: Devpost project galleries and project pages (each project page's "Winner" list, "Built With" tags and "Try it out" links were scraped directly), Microsoft Education Blog winners post_

## 1. Scope
**Method.** I pulled every Winner-badged entry from each gallery below with curl, opened each winner's project page, and kept projects whose prize name was an education prize (Education Track, Best Education Hack, Best Use of AI in Education, EdTech, and similar). About 1,600 winner pages were checked. Stacks and repos come from the project pages.

**Galleries scraped (`https://<slug>.devpost.com/project-gallery`):** treehacks-2023/2024/2025/2026, la-hacks-2023/2024/2025/2026, hackprinceton-fall-2023, -spring-2024, -spring-2025, -fall-2025, dubhacks-23, dubhacks24, hackthenorth2023/2024/2025, pennapps-xxiv/xxv/xxvi, hackgt-x, hackgt-12, hacktx-2023/2024, hackdavis-2024/2025/2026, sfhacks-2024, citrus-hack-2025, shellhacks-2023, shellhacks2024, shellhacks2025, hacklytics-2024/2026, technica-2023/2024/2025, hackharvard-2023/2024/2025, cal-hacks-11-0, cal-hacks-12-0, mhacks-2024, mhacks-16, hackutd-2025, hackumass-xi/xii, knight-hacks-vi/vii, nwhacks-2024/2025, uofthacks-11, hacknyu-2025, hackru-spring-2024, boilermake-xi/xii, swamphacks-xi, deltahacks-xi, qhacks-2025, hackillinois-2024/2025, hackpsu-fall-2024, vandyhacks-x, and microsoftaiclassroom (an online, education-only Devpost event; winners also confirmed at https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/education/blog/?p=5318).

## 3. Patterns
**1. Plain "upload notes → quizzes/flashcards" tools win only at small or education-only events.** Cuepal, ChatEDU and Study Conquest won at the Microsoft online contest and at Technica, where most entries are education projects. At the big flagship events (TreeHacks, LA Hacks, HackPrinceton), education winners added one of the hooks below.

**2. Winners put the AI inside the live classroom.** TreeHacks has given its education prize to classroom-embedded tools three times: Zazu in 2023 (TA in a video call), and TA-DA and Prereq in 2026 (Zoom confusion detection, live lecture knowledge graphs). AskAnon (anonymous questions in lecture) and Smart Office Hub (office-hours queue) follow the same pattern. These tools help the teacher as well as the student, which sets them apart from solo chatbots.

**3. A new modality or medium beats another chat box.** Examples: a VR math visualizer (4Desmos), AI-generated video lessons (VidCraft), a HeyGen avatar tutor (Minerva, which won two prizes), an AI-character history game (Dialogues Through Time), an interactive visual note space (opennote, two prizes), a learning game (Succession), and a neural-net playground (Restless Learning). Judges reward a visible "wow" in a 3-minute demo.

**4. Computer-vision sign-language learning keeps winning.** Excel ASL (Technica 2025), Msign (MHacks 2024) and ASL Learn (BoilerMake XI) all won education tracks with MediaPipe, Roboflow or TensorFlow hand tracking. It's a reliable formula, but judges have now seen it many times, so it needs a twist.

**5. A specific user and a specific pain point.** Winners name a narrow audience: students with ADHD (Study Conquest), civil-service exam takers (SF Civil Pro), ESL lecture attendees, students who code by voice (Project Lyla), and financial-aid applicants (ArcAid, which also uses ZK privacy). This specificity separates them from the many generic "ChatGPT for studying" entries.

**6. Education tracks are broad.** HackPrinceton's "Best Education Hack" went to a résumé tailor (Resume ArchiTech) and LA Hacks' hack2school to an AI dev-agent pair (Devin & Kevin). Judges read career skills, developer learning and accessibility as education. Several education winners also took a sponsor prize (Dolby.io, HeyGen, Bloomberg, Simplify, DAIN, Wolfram), so they stacked prizes.

**Recurring tech.** OpenAI or Claude or Gemini plus Next.js/React is the default. The standouts add a live-data integration (Zoom, Canvas, arXiv, Google Calendar), a knowledge graph or RAG layer (Prereq, HiveMind, Project Lyla), or an output beyond text (video, VR, avatar, voice through ElevenLabs).

**Takeaways for our Knight Hacks project**
1. Don't submit a generic AI tutor. Put the AI where learning already happens, such as a live lecture, a Canvas course or office hours, and show it working in real time. TreeHacks 2026 gave all three Zoom Education Track prizes to tools like this.
2. Pick one named learner with one painful moment (for example, "UCF CS student lost in a COP 3502 lecture"). Make the demo that moment, and back it with a statistic in the pitch.
3. Make the output something other than chat text: a knowledge graph, generated video or avatar, an AR/VR visualization, or voice. That gives the 3-minute demo its wow.
4. Design for prize stacking. Pick the sponsor API (Zoom, HeyGen, ElevenLabs, Gemini or MLH's "Best Use of AI in Education") so one build qualifies for the education track and one or two sponsor prizes.
5. Teacher-side value counts. Tools that help instructors (TA-DA, Zazu, Smart Office Hub) score as well as student tools and are less crowded.
