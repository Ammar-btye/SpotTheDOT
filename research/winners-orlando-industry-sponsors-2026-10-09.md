## 3. Patterns

- **Industrial sponsors set narrow operational problems and reward literal answers.** All three Siemens Energy winners at HackUPC built the same thing, a data-center designer, and were ranked by depth: FlowCore added LP optimization and MQTT live simulation. All three NextEra drone winners at Knight Hacks VIII used OR-Tools routing. The winners did not reinterpret the prompt; they solved it fully and showed it running.
- **Security and asset-risk prompts dominate the 2024–2026 industrial challenges.** Examples: Siemens at hackaTUM in both years (PrismX, RiskFlow, Antibody, CaraKube), NextEra's 2026 malware challenge, and CACI's spyware prize. Winners chain together LLM extraction, a graph database (Neo4j) or sandbox, a risk score, and an auto-generated remediation report or fix. "Detect, explain, act" is the pattern.
- **Defense primes at student events often give soft, community prompts, and polished, empathetic UX wins those.** L3Harris asked for STEM outreach to kids (2023) and freshman onboarding (2024). The winners were friendly React/Firebase/Unity apps with a mascot or game layer: ToborFairy, STEMTrek, Sorcerer's Sprouts. Booz Allen's "Best Social Hack" went to an accessibility review site (Play More).
- **When a prompt is open-ended, the operator-safety angle wins.** Lockheed's open challenge went to EyeAlert, which detects fatigue in high-stakes operators with on-device CV, and to aerial ML (SayNoToTrash). At TreeHacks 2025, Terra's military-framed Healthcare prize also went to a fighter-pilot performance project, Project AVIA (https://devpost.com/software/project-avia-autonomous-vigilance-insights-for-aviation). Human-in-the-loop safety for pilots and operators is a strong frame for defense judges.
- **Showing hardware and XR helps with simulation-minded sponsors.** Thomas Tracks XR (HoloLens/Unity) won Siemens rail, and Aviateur (iOS AR scans) placed with American Airlines. Orlando's simulation sector (Lockheed MFC, Siemens Energy and the PEO STRI corridor) is likely to respond to this too.
- **Recurring stack:** Python + FastAPI backends, Next.js/React frontends, Gemini or OpenAI for extraction, OR-Tools for routing, Neo4j for asset graphs, Unity for games and XR, and Firebase for quick apps.
- **Prizes at these challenges are modest** (gift cards, swag, interviews). NextEra's 1st place is a guaranteed interview, so the judges are effectively recruiters and reward engineering rigor and clear explanation.

**Takeaways for our Knight Hacks project**
1. Plan for NextEra (it sponsored Knight Hacks VII and VIII and HackUSF 2026). The likely prompts are grid operations, optimization or cyber. Prepare reusable modules ahead of time: OR-Tools VRP plus a Mapbox dashboard, and an LLM-plus-sandbox security triage pipeline.
2. If an industrial sponsor (Siemens Energy, Lockheed, L3Harris) posts a narrow operational prompt, answer it literally. Then add one standout layer: optimization math, live simulation, or AR visualization, with quantified results.
3. For any security-flavored prompt, build the full "ingest → graph or sandbox → risk score → auto-remediation report" loop. Every recent Siemens and NextEra security winner did.
4. If a defense prime posts a community or STEM prompt, polish and empathy win. Use a mascot or game layer, a clear user persona, and a Figma-quality UI.
5. Frame projects around operator safety and decision support (fatigue, inspection, maintenance). This fits Orlando's defense, simulation, theme-park and airline employers.
