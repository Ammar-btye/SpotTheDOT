## 3. Patterns

1. **Hyper-local climate risk wins.** Chilladelphia (Philly heat by address), U-Plan (urban heat islands), StormSurge (post-storm map) and Bitcamp Brawlers (disaster to mortgage delinquency) all scope a global problem down to a street or a household. Judges reward "type your address, see your risk."
2. **Real public Earth data is the moat.** Winners name their datasets: Google Earth Engine (WaterView, FarmShield), USGS (WaterView), NASA AppEEARS/MODIS (Cropora), NASA Landsat (PyroGuard), Open-Meteo (ERWIN), GraphCast/Earth2Studio (Morro). A climate project with only mock data rarely shows up among winners.
3. **Hardware and edge make disaster demos credible.** These include an Arduino compost sensor (WasteWise), ESP8266 + satellite (Canary), a LoRa mesh (Meshworks), Jetson grid monitoring (GridVeda), a Raspberry Pi (Drip) and drones + smart glasses (PUSHPA). Disaster scenarios assume the network is down, and offline or edge designs answer that directly.
4. **Prize stacking via sponsor tech.** Many winners took a sustainability prize plus a sponsor prize:
   - SaR 3D: Sustainability + Cloudflare
   - Hexagon: SLB + MATLAB
   - Green Habits: three prizes, including Auth0
   - Spark: three prizes, including Convex

   Corporate climate-adjacent sponsors run their own tracks: Bloomberg at PennApps, SLB and Chevron at HackRice, Fannie Mae at Bitcamp, Skylo at Cal Hacks, Waymo at ShellHacks, and NextEra at Knight Hacks.
5. **Venture framing matters at top events.** TreeHacks' Stanford Ecopreneurship prizes are judged on user pain point, prototyping process, and regulatory/competitive context (ERWIN, GridVeda, Morro, Spark). The pitch must name a buyer (grid operators, carbon-credit buyers, farmers).
6. **"AI's own footprint" is a fresh, winning meta-angle.** Drip (AI water use) and CallToChange (LLM carbon) both won at LA Hacks, and Token Whale won Hackabull 2025 overall (logged in the Florida file).
7. **Saturated ideas still win small tracks but not overall.** Waste-sorting computer vision (EcoSort), carbon-footprint trackers (Green Habits, Sustainable Earth) and food-waste pantries (PantryPuzzle) win track prizes at mid-size events. At big events, the 2024–2026 winners are more technical: geochemistry, weather models, edge AI and 3D reconstruction.
8. **Disaster ideas recur in Florida.** Hurricanes, floods and evacuation come up again and again at ShellHacks, Knight Hacks and HackRU (PyroGuard, StormSurge, plus FloodGuard and hurrycane in other files). There is room for one that is better grounded in data.

**Takeaways for our Knight Hacks project**
- **Go Orlando-specific.** Use address-level hurricane, flood or heat risk for Central Florida with real feeds: NOAA/NHC advisories, USGS stream gauges, FEMA flood zones, Open-Meteo. Make the demo "enter your UCF-area address."
- **Expect an energy sponsor.** NextEra/FPL has run Knight Hacks challenges two years running (energy efficiency in VII, drone grid inspection in VIII). A grid-resilience or outage-and-restoration angle could hit both the sponsor track and a sustainability track.
- **Add one physical or edge element.** An ESP32 sensor, an offline mesh or on-device inference makes a disaster demo believable when the network is down.
- **Stack prizes on purpose.** Build the core on the MLH sponsor stack (Gemini, Cloudflare, MongoDB, DigitalOcean, Snowflake) so one project qualifies for several sponsor prizes.
- **Pitch like a venture.** Name the buyer (county emergency management, utility, insurer, homeowner), give one hard number, and avoid generic waste-sorting or carbon-tracker ideas.
