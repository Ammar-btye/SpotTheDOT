## 3. Patterns
**Sponsor prompts are concrete and repeat every year. A team can prepare for them in advance.**
- **Toyota:** at TAMUhack 2025 the prompt was a car-shopping app with finance and lease costs. HackTX 2025 asked for a TFS finance and lease simulator; HackUTD 2025 asked for "find your dream Toyota." TAMUhack 2026 wanted a recommendation engine with semantic search, constraint relaxation and zero-match fallbacks. Winners: Kaizen, FiYota, Toyotron, RideIQ.
- **American Airlines:** the prompt is always "passenger experience, employee experience, or operational efficiencies." The top prizes went to operations and crew tools (AIsle0, FindAir) and voice concierges (Aria, TalkTuahAirline).
- **NorthMark Compute & Cloud:** asks you to make HPC usable by non-experts. At HackTX 2025 the prompt was F1 race strategy; at HackUTD 2025 it was data-center technicians. Winners: Constellation, Guido, DataDriver. HPC Runner placed 2nd overall at TAMUhack 2026 on the same theme.
- **Capital One:** "Best Financial Hack", appearing at almost every event, with the optional Nessie API.
- **Others:** State Farm and Goldman Sachs at HackUTD tend to want B2B tools (API regression testing, vendor onboarding, risk). The 2025 HackUTD prompts were in the hacker pack, not on Devpost, so they are inferred here from the winners.

**What won:**
1. **Voice agents.** ElevenLabs or Retell in front of an LLM (TalkTuahAirline, TalkTuahBank, Aria, Toyotron, Haggle). One voice agent often collects an overall prize and a sponsor prize at the same time.
2. **Multi-agent tools aimed at the sponsor's own employees** (DataDriver, InsighT, UpGuardian, AIsle0). HackUTD sponsors clearly rewarded internal-ops tools over consumer apps.
3. **Physical or multimodal demos win overall prizes:** wands tracked by computer vision (AstroDoodle), face reading (Poker Face, Haggle), AR (cortex), a robot medic (MediBot), a turret (C-AAM). Of the six grand prizes logged (HackTX 2023–25, HackRice 16, TAMUhack 2026 Software and Hardware), five have something physical or on-camera in the demo. The exception is AIAIO.
4. **Prize stacking is common:** TalkTuahAirline (overall plus AA), TalkTuahBank (General plus Goldman), Toyotron (Toyota plus NVIDIA), Asset Angel (General plus State Farm). These teams built one strong core and framed it so it qualified for two tracks.
5. **Recurring tech:** Gemini and ElevenLabs dominate 2025–26. NVIDIA Nemotron appeared in many HackUTD 2025 sponsor winners (Toyotron, UpGuardian, DataDriver, TrustIssues.AI) because NVIDIA ran its own track. MCP and agent frameworks (LangGraph, Google ADK, AgentIQ) show up in the HPC and enterprise tracks. Next.js is the default front end.
6. **The theme matters at HackTX:** there were Best Celestial-Themed and Best Arcade-Themed prizes, and the 2025 Best Overall winner was itself space-themed.
7. **HackTX 2025 added "Best Human Intelligence"** (no AI at the core), which signals that judges are tired of LLM wrappers.

**Takeaways for our Knight Hacks project**
1. Target one corporate sponsor prompt and make the project credible to their employees. Internal-ops tools (crew, technician, PM, underwriting) beat consumer novelty with HackUTD-style sponsors, and the prompts repeat year to year.
2. Design for prize stacking: one core that fits a sponsor track and an MLH tech prize (Gemini, ElevenLabs, Vultr, Auth0) and is good enough for overall.
3. Put a live voice or camera interaction in the demo. Real-time multimodal input is what took the overall prizes.
4. If the theme is HPC or data, put an agent or MCP layer over complex infrastructure so a non-expert can drive it. The pattern swept NorthMark three times.
5. Use the sponsor's API or data where one exists (Nessie, the AA flight engine, Toyota inventory) and say so in the Devpost write-up.
