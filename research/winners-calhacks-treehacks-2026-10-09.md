# Winners: Cal Hacks & TreeHacks (incl. UC Berkeley AI Hackathon)
_Researched 2026-10-09 · sources: Devpost galleries and project pages (badges plus the "Submitted to → Winner" prize list), Stanford Daily TreeHacks recaps (2024, 2025, 2026)_

## 1. Scope
I scraped the winner-badged entries from 10 Devpost galleries (502 winning projects in all) and read every winner's project page for prizes, Built With tags and Try-it-out links:
- Cal Hacks 10.0 (2023): https://cal-hacks-10.devpost.com/project-gallery (40 winners). The `cal-hacks-10-0` slug returns 403.
- Cal Hacks 11.0 (2024): https://cal-hacks-11-0.devpost.com/project-gallery (62)
- Cal Hacks 12.0 (2025): https://cal-hacks-12-0.devpost.com/project-gallery (102)
- TreeHacks 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026: https://treehacks-20XX.devpost.com/project-gallery (50, 60, 70 and 64)
- UC Berkeley AI Hackathon, run by Cal Hacks (2023, 2024, 2025): https://uc-berkeley-ai-hackathon.devpost.com, https://uc-berkeley-ai-hackathon-2024.devpost.com, https://uc-berkeley-ai-hackathon-2025.devpost.com (16, 18 and 20)
- Organizer and press confirmation: https://stanforddaily.com/2024/02/27/treehacks-2024/, https://stanforddaily.com/2025/02/18/treehacks-awards-200000-in-prizes-to-students-from-around-the-world/, https://stanforddaily.com/2026/02/15/12th-annual-treehacks/

I logged only organizer-level prizes: overall and grand prizes, track grand prizes, "Most Technically Complex", and a few top AI-lab sponsor tracks. I skipped the roughly 450 sponsor-only winners. WebFetch gets a 403 from Devpost, so I fetched the pages with curl.

## 3. Patterns
**1. Hardware + AI wins grand prizes far more often than its share of entries.** Only about 33 of the 502 winners carry microcontroller tags (Arduino, ESP32, Raspberry Pi or Jetson). Yet 4 of the 7 TreeHacks grand-prize placings from 2024 to 2026 went to hardware projects (Baymax, Portable Braille, Shepherd, Robosurge), and hardware projects took Cal Hacks 11 1st (Duet, EEG) and Cal Hacks 12 2nd (Ted.AI). Software projects still win plenty of top prizes: HawkWatch, Supernova, FaceTimeOS, and every Berkeley AI Hackathon grand prize. A physical device gives judges a memorable live demo.

**2. Accessibility and care for vulnerable people is the most common winning theme.** It appears in Shepherd, Baymax, Edith, Portable Braille, Mira, Ted.AI, Dispatch AI and Haven, and in earlier winners such as Smart Shoe (2023) and Good Samaritan (2024). The Stanford Daily credited Baymax's win to the pairing of "innovation and empathy" (stanforddaily.com/2024/02/27/treehacks-2024/). TreeHacks judges on creativity, technical complexity and social impact (Stanford Daily, 2026).

**3. Winners build the hard part themselves instead of wrapping an API.** Baymax and Robosurge wrote their own inverse kinematics. Shepherd built its own LiDAR steering engine with EMA smoothing and a BLE protocol. Edith ran inference across 4 A100s. ChromaChord built on music-theory papers. "Most Technically Complex" goes to systems-level work like dockerc (Zig), Freak in the Sheets (LLVM backend) and Proactive Refresh (threshold cryptography). Repeat winners make this visible: kognise also won Cal Hacks 11 Most Entertaining with a language inside Figma, and NilsIrl also won TreeHacks 2025 Most Creative with the OmNom robot.

**4. The stacks shifted from OpenAI single calls (2023) to multi-agent, multi-model systems (2025–26).** Across all 2023 winners in these galleries the top tags were python, react, openai, flask and convex. In 2025–26 they were python, typescript, react, **claude (48 of 186)**, fastapi, next.js, vercel, gemini, fetch.ai, groq, vapi and letta. FaceTimeOS, Project No Cap and Supernova each chain 5–10 sponsor tools behind one orchestrator, so they stack sponsor prizes as well.

**5. Voice and real-time interaction are everywhere.** Dispatch AI (Retell + Hume), FaceTimeOS (FaceTime as the interface), GhostWriter (Deepgram live transcription), Mira and Nexus all win with low-latency, conversational demos rather than dashboards.

**6. Stacking sponsor prizes is normal.** ShipSense won 4 prizes, and Haven and Shepherd won 3 each. Agent-platform prizes (Fetch.ai, Letta, YC "Build an Iconic YC Company") hand out many awards per event: Cal Hacks 11 gave out 8 Fetch.ai Agentic awards, and TreeHacks 2026 named 6 YC 1st-place winners.

**Takeaways for our Knight Hacks project**
1. Build AI that acts in the physical world: a cheap sensor or actuator (ESP32, phone LiDAR or camera, smart glasses) plus a model, demoed live. This is overrepresented among TreeHacks grand prizes. If we stay software-only, aim for a live, real-time agent demo like FaceTimeOS or HawkWatch.
2. Pick a concrete, sympathetic user (blind, elderly, patients, first responders) and show measurable impact, for example "1/20th the cost of existing devices" (Shepherd).
3. Hand-build one hard technical core (control loop, custom pipeline, model optimization) and say so explicitly in the Devpost "How we built it". Judges reward depth over API glue.
4. Use one agent orchestrator (Claude/Gemini) with voice I/O, then plug in 2–3 sponsor tools where they genuinely fit, so you can stack track prizes on top of the overall.
5. Make the demo real-time and conversational. Latency is part of the "wow".
