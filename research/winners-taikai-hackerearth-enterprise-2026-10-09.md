## 3. Patterns

- **Numbers beat flash at sponsor-run events.** The Nemotron Seoul judges said Nota's PASCAL-MoE won because it "demonstrated not only qualitative but also quantitative evaluation," unlike flashier demos with no metrics. AMD's kernel contests were judged purely on benchmarks: any submission slower than the PyTorch baseline scored zero. RadeonFlow won both rounds, and Chaos Economy's headline claim was "3B beats 120B." AMD and NVIDIA juries reward a measured delta.
- **The sponsor's newest product sits at the center of the project.** Every NeMo winner was built around the Agent Toolkit, and the #1 entry stacked four NVIDIA products (cuOpt, Omniverse, Jetson, NeMo). The UCSC tracks were literally named after NemoClaw and DGX Spark, and the organizer said the goal was building "on something that's so brand new." The Databricks World Cup winners each listed four or five platform features (Model Serving, Vector Search, Apps, MLflow, Unity Catalog).
- **Enterprise and vertical use cases beat general chatbots.** Examples: building-code compliance (ReguBEAM), legal RAG (AITHENA), biotech screening (AdaptiveFilters), fleet routing (Route Optimization Agent), vehicle diagnostics, DevRel and data access (MCP Enterprise track), and factory robots (FactoryMind). The Gradio MCP hackathon even created a separate "Best Enterprise" award, which MCEPTION won.
- **Safety and governance layers win "best overall."** Examples include Cite-Before-Act (human-in-the-loop middleware for MCP), MonkeyClaw (agent-ecosystem security), OpenCodeReview (vulnerability detection) and Automato (human-in-the-loop RPA).
- **Energy and grid problems get a tangible-device or forecasting answer.** NextEra's FSU prompt ("cut household energy use 10%") was won by hardware plus an app with real-time data (Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D, ShadE, AEGIS). The Databricks 2nd-place project predicted grid failures from space weather, with recommended operator actions.
- **Agents with real-world actuation stand out.** Examples: forklift routing on Jetson, robot bartender (Megas XLR), drone and vehicle MCP servers, hydroponics agent (HydroClaw).
- **TAIKAI and HackerEarth are poor sources for this.** Their results pages render client-side or are empty, and TAIKAI's events are mostly web3. The sponsor's own blog is the reliable record.

**Takeaways for our Knight Hacks project**
1. Put one hard, measured number in the demo and on a slide (latency, throughput, cost, accuracy, or "X% less energy"), with a baseline beside it. Corporate judges at AMD, NVIDIA and Nota explicitly favored quantitative evaluation over polish.
2. Build the core of the project on the sponsor's newest tool, not a side integration. For AMD, run inference or fine-tuning on ROCm/MI300X Developer Cloud. For NVIDIA, use Nemotron, NIM or the NeMo Agent Toolkit. For Databricks, use Apps, Vector Search, MLflow and Unity Catalog together. Name each component on the architecture slide.
3. Pick a vertical, enterprise-shaped problem with a clear buyer (a utility operator, a compliance officer, a fleet manager) and show the business case. For NextEra, a grid or outage forecast that recommends actions, or a home energy device, mirrors past winners.
4. Add a human-in-the-loop, safety or audit layer to any agent. It has repeatedly taken "Best Overall" and security-flavored awards.
5. If hardware is available, close the loop physically (sensor, robot or drone). Actuation was over-represented among the NVIDIA, Hugging Face and NextEra winners.
