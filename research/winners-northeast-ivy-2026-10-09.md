## 3. Patterns
- **Healthcare and accessibility own the top spot.** At least 9 of the 30 rows are health or accessibility projects: SoundSheild, SurgeVue, PSP, SixthSense, GaitGuard, LifeSaver, Fluxus, Fivi and EyeCraft. Most frame a specific, sympathetic user, such as autistic listeners, blind pedestrians, post-op patients or players with limited mobility. Patient-safety challenge prizes recur at PennApps XXV, HackHarvard 2024, HackPrinceton S25 and Hack@Brown 2025, so health is a reliable second shot at a prize.
- **Physical hardware wins overall far more often than its share of submissions.** About 9 of 36 overall or top-3 placings use Arduino, ESP32 or Raspberry Pi: SoundSheild, SurgeVue, PSP, ResQme, SixthSense, Circa, GaitGuard, Sortacle and Diabeteasy. Hardware gives judges a live demo, and these entries pair cheap sensors with an ML or LLM layer rather than shipping hardware alone.
- **Camera-based interaction is the most common "wow" technique.** About 10 of 36 top placings use webcam CV, MediaPipe pose or hands, or YOLO: kinemo, MindPad, LifeSaver, SurgeVue, Sortacle and YGroove. Using the body as the controller won overall twice (kinemo, MindPad) and took a HackMIT track (EyeCraft).
- **Voice is a cheap layer of polish.** ElevenLabs shows up in 6 of the overall winners (CharactAR, TeleSpeech, Tidbits, HealthyTales, LifeSaver, Yumi), and Fivi used OpenAI's voice API. Judges reward a project that talks back.
- **Generic LLM wrappers rarely place.** The overall winners that rely on LLMs add something on top: multi-agent orchestration (Sustain-ify with CrewAI and Groq), a VLM over imagery (Garuda), geospatial or satellite data (Watt's Up with NASA POWER and SegFormer, OverSEA, TerraLink) or synthetic media (Rally AI, Tidbits). Plain chat apps mostly win small sponsor prizes such as Wolfram, .Tech or GoDaddy domains.
- **Sponsor tracks reward the sponsor's own problem.**
  - The Visa "Truth and Trust" track at HackHarvard 2025 went to fraud and MFA projects (Visa Verify, Veritas, C2Pay, freak-cha).
  - Capital One finance prizes appear at nearly every event (Plate-O, DealyDigest, Roots, SpaceShare).
  - HackPrinceton Fall 2025 handed out stackable sponsor prizes (Dedalus Labs, xAI Grok, Knot TransactionLink, Photon, YC challenge), and many winners stacked 2–3 of them; edgecart won Capital One, YC and Knot at once.
- **Usual web stack.** Next.js (15 of 36 top placings) with a FastAPI or Flask backend (10 of 36). MongoDB and Supabase are the usual stores.

**Takeaways for our Knight Hacks project**
1. Pick one concrete user with a health, safety or accessibility need, and make the demo physical or camera-driven. An ESP32 or Raspberry Pi with sensors, or MediaPipe and YOLO on a webcam, plus an AI layer is the pattern that keeps winning first place.
2. Make the demo multimodal: add voice output (ElevenLabs or a realtime voice API) and gesture or vision input. Live interaction beat dashboards at every event in this set.
3. Give the LLM real data or a real actuator: satellite or NASA data, sensors, a VLM over images, or agents that act. Don't ship a chat box.
4. Plan to stack prizes. Design the core so that 2–3 sponsor APIs (MLH Gemini/Vultr, Capital One, any fintech or LLM sponsor) are meaningfully in the loop. Most overall winners also took a track or sponsor prize.
5. Ship a Devpost page with a clear one-line user problem and a public GitHub repo. Almost every winner here links one.
